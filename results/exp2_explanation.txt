EXPERIMENT 2 — LEARNING RATE (α) COMPARISON
=============================================
We compare three alpha values (deliverable: >=3):
  α = 0.01 (small), 0.1 (medium), 0.3 (large)

Alpha controls the step size of each Q-value update:
  Q(s,a) ← Q(s,a) + α × [target - Q(s,a)]

Small α (0.01): learns slowly but stably.
  Each experience has little influence — needs many episodes.

Medium α (0.1): balanced speed and stability.
  Recommended default for deterministic environments.

Large α (0.3): learns fast but can overshoot.
  Recent experience dominates; may oscillate around optimum.

Theory justification (from lecture):
In a deterministic environment (our museum), each observation is
reliable so larger alpha is safe. In stochastic environments a
smaller alpha averages out noise — but here 0.1 is the sweet spot.
